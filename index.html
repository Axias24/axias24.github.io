About

I am a Ph.D. candidate in Economics at the University of Maryland. My research is in empirical industrial organization. I estimate structural models of demand and firm behavior in retail markets for consumer packaged goods, with a focus on how well these models predict substitution and on what data can discipline them. I also work on causal inference for large-scale experiments, most recently as an economist intern at Amazon Ads.

My job market paper uses the 2015 withdrawal of Blue Bell, a regional ice cream brand, to test how well demand models estimated before the recall predict how much of a removed brand’s demand rivals retain and which rivals receive it. Where private label sits in the nesting structure matters more for these predictions than how much taste heterogeneity a model allows, a lesson for merger review and for valuing product variety. In other work, I study economies of scope in retail distribution with Ying Fan, Yidan Xu, and Chenyu Yang.

Before Maryland, I completed an M.A. in Economics at Columbia University and a B.S. in Economics and Mathematics at the University of California San Diego.

Experience

Summer 2026

Economist Intern, Amazon Ads

Marketing Finance · Seattle, WA

Evaluated five randomized promotions in an advertising-credit program and showed that credits raised seller ad adoption without incremental ad revenue, with returns varying widely across segments. I estimated intent-to-treat effects with covariate-adjusted regressions and used difference-in-differences and event studies for dynamics under repeated offers. Effects fade with repeated offers and concentrate among sellers with high predicted take-up (causal forests, compliance-score matching, double machine learning), which supports capped and targeted offers. I wrote the program’s technical review, briefed the Ads finance director, and recommended changes to the credit menu and re-offer policy plus a follow-on experiment.
